## First, in P4a's commit (the P3b review, ruling 1)

**The sync guide's memory sentence must not claim more than the mechanism.** P3b measured 16.0 MB for 10,000 patches, against
the 12 MB floor, and attributed the 4 MB to per-object metadata (about 400 B per object) by inference. The sentence says
memory follows the largest object, and it must also say "plus a few hundred bytes per object". **Measure the per-object figure**
with P3's live-bytes counter, or the probe allocator. Do not infer it, and state it in the CHANGELOG entry too.

## P4a: what the decode copies, and what borrowing it would cost (nothing built)

**Where a large object's copies are today** (P3a §1, the probe allocator, one 100 MiB file; `bundle import` and `sync accept`
are now equal at about 405 MB):
- the frame body read from the source;
- the decoded `ObjectEnvelope`, whose payload is copied out of the frame: **this is P4's copy;**
- the store's write path (`encode_envelope_file`: the encoded frame, the record body, the append buffer). P3a saw it twice
  in `bundle import`. That is RFC 158's and P5's, not P4's.

**P4a delivers:**
1. **The copies, by site, for one 100 MiB file,** on `bundle import`, `sync accept`, `bundle preview` and `verify`. These are
   the readers that decode a frame. Confirm which copy is the decode's, and its size.
2. **The design options for a borrowed decode, without `unsafe`:**
   - `ObjectEnvelope` holding its payload as `Cow<'a, [u8]>` (or a `Bytes`-like shared buffer) over the frame body;
   - a borrowed view type (`ObjectEnvelopeRef<'a>`) used only on the read-validate-write path;
   - decoding into the frame's own buffer, by taking ownership of the frame body and slicing it (a `Vec` split or a
     reference-counted buffer). This may need no lifetime at all.

   For each, give:
   - what it changes across crates: `prikk-object`'s public API (a published crate), the call sites, and the hashing and
     signature checks that read the payload;
   - **whether the validation is literally the same code** over borrowed and owned bytes (condition 2). A second
     validation path for borrowed bytes fails the condition;
   - the expected saving: one copy, so about 100 MB of the 405 MB for one 100 MiB file. Measured where you can (a probe),
     estimated where you cannot, saying which.
3. **The case for not building it.** Weigh these honestly against the saving:
   - the diff;
   - a new lifetime or type in a published crate;
   - RFC 158's Stage B, which will rework how large objects are held and may make a borrowed envelope moot.

   If the saving is one copy of one large object, and Stage B removes the reason, say so.

**Stop at P4a's report.** The architect rules: build one option, or close P4 as "not built, by design", with the numbers on
RFC 158's row.
