## 2. A read-gate row for a cold commit between checkpoints (about 30 min)

**Today:** the "one-file commit" row is `Flat`, measured right at a checkpoint, the best case. External review 029 (5.1)
showed that a cold commit between checkpoints costs as much as the content added since the nearest one, up to 63
blocks. 0.53.0's notes give the table. **Nothing in the gate catches a regression there.**

**Wanted:** a new row, `"one-file commit (since checkpoint)"`:
- **The fixture:**
  - sealed history whose nearest checkpoint lies a fixed number of blocks behind the tip (for example 32), with the
    `lifecycle-state.v1` cache removed so that the commit is cold;
  - the two stores differ only in **the content added after the checkpoint** (for example 1 MiB against 16 MiB in
    total);
  - say from `checkpoint_due` (`block_state.rs`) how you know where the checkpoint falls.
- **Held as `FollowsContent`,** naming 029 5.1 in its `finding`:
  - a ceiling of 1.5× your measurement;
  - a floor that promotes the row to `Flat` if a later release reads those files' kinds from their patches.
- **A budget, from 0.53.0 P1b's lesson** (a 3,200-commit fixture timed out `msrv-1.85.0` at 20 minutes): build the
  fixture in debug, report its wall time, and keep the whole gate's added time under 60 s in debug. If it cannot be,
  shrink the fixture and say how.
- **Controls:**
  - **perturb the anchor away,** so the cold commit full-replays from genesis: **the ceiling must fail.** For that,
    the history before the checkpoint must carry enough content of its own (the same in both stores) that a full
    replay reads well past 1.5× the anchored figure. Size it so, and show the row red;
  - **show the floor** by a measurement that reads flat (for example, a fixture with no content added after the
    checkpoint) promoting the row.

**No output change, so no CHANGELOG entry.** A test-only row.
