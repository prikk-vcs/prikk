## Part S0: `sync accept`'s claim write is checked like the other publish points (30 min, stop at 60)

**From the P2b review:** the claims are what `sync pending` lists. `sync.md` writes "claims and tags last" so that content
no claim names is not accepted. So the claims-and-tags write is a visibility point, a member of P2b's class.

**The rule:** `check_held_directories()` runs once, before the first claim or tag write of `sync accept`.
- **A store test:** a held directory moved before that write gives the P2b refusal. The claim and tag containers stay
  byte-identical, and `sync pending` is unchanged.
- **The red control:** the call removed, and that test goes red.
- **Docs:** `concurrency-locking.md`'s list of the places that check gains `sync accept`.
- **CHANGELOG:** fold it into P2b's entry.

**The promise to planeter binds this part.** `accept`'s write order stays patches and blobs, then AUTHOR key material,
then claims and tags. A refusal is exit 1. State both in the report.

## Part S1: measure, inventory, options (90 min, stop at 180). Nothing is built

**What the architect measured on the published 0.57.0** (`~/.pgtmp/s-probes/`: the scripts and their logs).

`probe18.sh`, planeter's shape (one small text edit per block, one claim per block, a fresh receiver):

| claims | `sync seal` store read | fsyncs | peak memory |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 250 | 343 MB | 14,205 | 12 MB |
| 1,000 | 5.2 GB | 57,825 | 11 MB |

That is quadratic: four times the claims cost 15 times the reads. `sync accept` reads 0.1–0.3 MB.

`probe17.sh`, one 1 MiB binary per block, then text edits:
- the first `sync seal` of the whole history reads 588 MB at 20 MiB and 8.8 GB at 100 MiB;
- a later round reads about 3× the history, while the source's own `seal` reads 37 MB.

**Two candidate sources, both in `seal_from_accepted.rs`.** The measurement must tell them apart:
- **(a) the batch simulation,** `simulate_seal_batch_target_manifest` (`:556` onwards). It calls `derive_candidate`
  "again, from scratch, on the whole grown list" for each claim, for the in-the-way check
  (`require_nothing_in_the_way_for_seal_batch`). Its doc names the incremental fold as "more efficient", and says why it
  was not used: `CandidateDerivation::state` is documented as test-only.
- **(b) the real per-claim loop,** `seal_from_accepted_claim_in_batch`. It "re-reads and re-derives everything", one
  `derive_candidate` per claim from that claim's parent. A receiver that has no checkpoint of its own may pay the whole
  history per claim. That is R's cause, met here once per claim.

**S1 delivers four things:**

1. **The split, measured.** Run planeter's shape at 250 and 1,000 claims, and at 10,000 if the 1,000 run's time allows
   (state the budget before starting; stop at twice it). Run probe17's shape at 20 and 100 MiB. Report for each:
   - store bytes read (`strace`, as the probes do);
   - P1's counter for fsyncs (files and directories apart);
   - peak memory;
   - (a) and (b) separately.

   One way to separate them is a test-only switch that skips the simulation. If you use it, say how, and show it is
   absent from release builds. Run on `main` (with P2), and the 0.57.0 numbers above are the baseline.
2. **The inventory of refusals (condition 2).** Every refusal the per-claim path can raise today, each with:
   - its `file:line`;
   - its message;
   - whether it comes before the first write, and at which claim.

   This includes the mid-batch case: `rfc116_sync_cli.rs::row6_a_mid_batch_seal_failure_stops_reports_and_leaves_the_earlier_seal_intact`
   (earlier claims' seals kept, the failure named at its claim). That behaviour is today's and stays.
3. **The crash table for an interrupted `sync seal` (condition 5), as it is today.** For each point between claims and
   within a claim's seal:
   - what is on disk;
   - what `verify`, `doctor` and `status` say;
   - what the next `sync seal` does.

   Today's tests are cited per row. A row with no test is marked so.
4. **The options (conditions 1, 3 and 4).** At least these, each with what it changes, the reads it would take on the
   measured shapes, and the risk to condition 1 (byte-identical output):
   - **(i) fold incrementally:** derive the pre-batch state once, and apply each claim's patches to one running state.
     The doc names it. It needs the state outside `block_state.rs`, so say what that does to `CandidateDerivation`'s
     documented test-only field.
   - **(ii) reuse RFC 159's mechanism** (`seal` without re-walking: what it records, and where a receiver lacks it).
     This is condition 4's "one mechanism". **Say how (i) and (ii) relate:** whether (ii) alone fixes (b), and whether
     (a) still needs (i).
   - **(iii)** anything else the measurement suggests.

   **Also say, from the P2a review, whether the per-claim loop should hold the object-store lock across its writes.**
   Today `objects.lock` is created and removed once per object write. Give that lock-order change's effect on the
   measured fsyncs, and its risk.

**Condition 1's test is designed now, not later.** A corpus comparison: for the same claims, the same block ids,
RefStates and published refs, byte for byte, before and after. Name the shapes it covers:
- text edits;
- large binaries;
- a merge in the history;
- renames;
- several refs;
- a received checkpoint;
- a mid-batch refusal.

Name how it runs: two builds, or a recorded expected corpus.

**Stop at S1's report.** The architect rules on the option, and the build handoff follows with the measured numbers as
its closing targets (250, 1,000 and 10,000 claims, reads linear in the claims).
